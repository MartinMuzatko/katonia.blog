I first stumbled upon the poetry of Lao Tzu – the Tao Te King (or Dao De Jing) – through the book Trust Chaos.

## The book that doesn't want to be understood

Much of it suggests that it's less about understanding something through logic, knowledge or wisdom than about sensing it intuitively.
This nature of the book also shows in the sheer number of different interpretations.

> Abandon wisdom, discard knowledge, <br />
> And people will benefit a hundredfold.

> Those who try to outshine others, <br />
> Dim their own light

This part speaks to me in particular. I'm so tired of watching friends and acquaintances try to feed their egos by talking about the great things they've bought or own. A downward spiral of consumption, compensation and emptiness. Often this behaviour is just a surrogate for what they truly long for. When I bring it up with these people, unexpected depths often open up. They place themselves above others, and those others can feel it. In doing so they deny themselves exactly what they are looking for: connection, belonging and closeness.

In her magnum opus "Finde deinen Fixstern", Vera F. Birkenbihl aptly calls this phenomenon the crowing rooster on the dung heap. A game people play, investing much of their energy into maintaining their self-image and status.

It reminds us not to focus only on how we are seen from the outside. Far more important is being at peace with yourself and knowing what you bring to the table.
Knowing who you want to be, and who you don't. That takes differentiation and integration: setting yourself apart through your abilities and quirks, owning them, and then integrating back into society with them.

Just as each part of our body has its own function so it can work together with the others, living beings have their own function too.
What kind of creature would it be if every organ strived to be like the heart, which so often gets the most credit for keeping us alive?

So it's worth becoming yourself, and not someone else.
Friedrich Nietzsche says as much in Ecce Homo: become who you are.

"The Courage To Be Disliked" by Fumitake Koga and Ichiro Kishimi uses the same metaphor:

> The greatest life-lie of all is to not live here and now. It is to look at the past and the future, <br />
> cast a dim light on one’s entire life, and believe that one has been able to see something. <br />
> Until now, you have turned away from the here and now and shone a light only on invented pasts and futures. <br />
> You have told a great lie to your life, to these irreplaceable moments.
