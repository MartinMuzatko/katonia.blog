How do you win friends? How do you get the job you want? How do you create a relationship? How do you get what you want?

Those are questions everybody asks.
Everybody wants to know how to be more successful, attractive, loved, valued and free.

It sets a condition.

Once I have this thing, I will finally be happy.<br />
Once I have this job, I will finally be fulfilled.<br />
Once I found my purpose, I can do what I'm meant to do.<br />
Once I have a partner, I can enjoy life.<br />

It robs you of any reason to be happy today. It makes you unable to enjoy today.
And thus, it makes you unattractive for what you yearn for.

Why is that?

The problem already relies in wanting. Because wanting directs your thoughts to the future, away from what you already have.
Away from what you can do here and now.

By thinking about the future, you quickly start to think about how you're not yet fit for it.
That you're not ready. It invites comparing yourself with others. By comparing yourself with others, you will feel inferior.

And so you become a self-loathing man of inaction. Especially when looking back how much time you already wasted by doing nothing and just wanting.

Depression is when you mourn the past and anxiety is what you have when you fear the future.

## How I failed miserably at wanting freedom

In November 2025, after I was fired, I finally decided: That's it. I want to become self-employed. I want to get financial and creative freedom. I'm sick of just bending over backwards for employers who wouldn't value me.

I had a new job offer, I already almost signed it, but I bravely decided to give this whole self-employment thing a try.

What followed was 5 months of playing games that industry insiders would advise you to play. Marketing, social media, more likes, more followers, build in public, build a personal brand, distribution.

I hated it. I made no progress at all. I neglected friends, didn't work out anymore, stayed at home all day alone. The harder I tried to grasp onto it, the further it moved into the future.

## But if wanting is not the solution, what is?

I'm glad you ask! There are two different ways of wanting and achieving.

One turns our survival modes on: fight, flight, freeze.<br />
The other healthier mode of engaging with life is play.

But this is serious! Why should I play when I so desperately need a partner, a job, the thing, my purpose?

Do you want a desperate, needy and clingy person as partner or employee? <br />
Or do you want to be with somebody who is flexible, dynamic, capable and playful?

When you don't respect your own boundaries, you make it easier for others to disregard them.
When you suppress your personality to please everyone, you make it harder for people to discover who you actually are.

And the same properties we value in others, we should start valueing in ourselves. <br />
Do you want to be competent, strong, self-reliant, generous, emotional stable, humorous?

All these values appear when we express them through action, not through thoughts alone.
You can't think yourself into the future, you have to start living it today.

And that makes you absolutely attractive.

Because attractiveness emerges when you stop desperately trying to become attractive. <br />
Situations become easy, when you start loosening your grip on them.

## Play

When you start being playful, curious, interested, you can demonstrate your value. You can live in the here and now and therefore you are able to discovered. Authenticity attracts the right people and repels the wrong people. If you hide away all the time, never dare to be your true self, never speak up, how can you expect to find the fitting opportunities?

You can compare that with when you were a child. When you learned to walk, you didn't compare yourself with others. You didn't even want to learn to walk, it just happened. You were curious, you experimented and you made mistakes - that's all part of the process.

Imagine what you could do today if you had the same playfulness? The same curiosity? Pure power!

## My way to freedom

After five months of hustling, I took a week off to rest with my parents.

Breathing the cold air of the Türnitzer Alps, I found myself skiing cross-country for the first time. A little shaky, a little clumsy, but genuinely curious - playful.

Something shifted. Away from the constant pressure of building a business, I finally had room to think again. To play, learn, connect with people and rediscover what actually mattered to me.

I realized I had been playing an entirely different game than the one I wanted to play all time along.

I don't want to feed the superficial marketing machine. I love deep conversations, intellectual exchange and genuine human connection. I want creative freedom and self-determination, not another hamster wheel.

And then, almost ironically, just as I was beginning to let go of the pressure, the phone rang. A potential client had a software development project for me and they needed a freelancer.

Being relaxed, present and genuinely myself, I managed to win the client in just 15 minutes.

The irony is bizzare. 5 months of stressing myself amounted to nothing. 15 Minutes of doing what I love gave me a new life of fortune.

Would I've been able to land that contract when I was stressed, insecure and desperate? I doubt it.
